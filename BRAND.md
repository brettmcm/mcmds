# MCM Design Co. Brand Taste

This document codifies the universal judgment layer of the MCM Design Co. system. Use `DESIGN.md` for measurable foundations. Use this document when deciding what kind of composition, rhythm, hierarchy, density, image logic, or visual emphasis a piece of work deserves.

Channel-specific production details belong in `CHANNELS/*.md`.

## Brand Thesis

MCM Design Co. should feel editorial, image-led, restrained, culturally sharp, and built with product-design rigor. The work should have enough taste to avoid template thinking and enough discipline to remain useful.

The system is not generic SaaS, not startup default, not decorative portfolio maximalism, and not a luxury-beige mood board. MCMDS-specific work may lean into dark technical atmosphere, operational surfaces, dashboards, and material detail, but it should still feel considered, tactile, useful, and alive through image, type, contrast, alignment, and negative space.

## Creative Principles

1. Let the subject lead. The composition should reveal the actual product, person, place, project, material, state, or decision.
2. Use restraint as an active decision. Sparse does not mean empty; it means each visible element has a job.
3. Prefer structure over ornament. Rules, alignment, spacing, type hierarchy, material, and image should solve most design problems.
4. Keep editorial mood and practical clarity together. A piece can feel sharp without hiding the information someone came for.
5. Preserve one personality across channels. A slide, print piece, web page, report, or social graphic should feel like the same system translated into a different medium.

## Product design judgment

Product surfaces should establish one dominant working area and demote surrounding context. Navigation, supporting panes, linked material, utilities, and status information should remain available without competing with the current task.

Navigation is conditional, not ceremonial. Do not add a left rail, menu, or application shell to a short linear task simply because a reference image includes one. Likewise, do not reproduce screenshot framing, window chrome, outer fields, or rounded application canvases unless the product itself requires them.

Use pane proportion and controlled asymmetry to communicate priority. A narrow navigation rail, a generous editor or task area, and a quieter context or utility region can create hierarchy without oversized cards or ornamental chrome. Persistent controls belong at stable edges or corners where repeated use makes their location predictable.

Treat imagery as an active product surface or evidence aperture. It may reveal the subject, orient the user, preview material, or create a bounded first-sight signal; it should not become a decorative backdrop behind controls that need precision.

For focused reviews, quotes, schedules, and other operational pages, prefer one full-width reading plane inside a bounded workspace. Avoid a repeated title rail/content-column split when it makes scanning busier. Group related line items and use whitespace to mark major changes in task or calculation; do not manufacture separation with cards, thick rules, or redundant subtotals.

Separate atmosphere from usability. Expressive image treatments, oversized display type, mono readouts, measurement marks, and dark technical effects should live in bounded regions. Navigation, controls, forms, and reading surfaces should remain legible, stable, and direct.

## MCMDS signal/evidence layer

For MCMDS-specific work, apply the creative direction in `CREATIVE-DIRECTION.md` as a visual overlay under the universal system. The territory is controlled signal in a dark field: document-grid structure, quiet metadata, technical material detail, nocturnal atmosphere, specific imagery, and scarce operational color.

Use this layer to make work feel more precise, artifact-led, technical, and tactile. Do not use it to override the role-based typography and casing rules, spacing, radius, accessibility, or containment.

Good MCMDS moves:

- Show evidence: product state, material detail, document artifact, exposed construction, dashboard state, source note, timestamp, coordinate, or decision.
- Use dark operational atmosphere when it supports the subject.
- Let one signal color carry status, heat, route, energy, focus, or interruption.
- Use thin rules, grids, apertures, cropped artifacts, and compact metadata for structure.
- Keep panels, boxes, and modules for repeated objects, dashboards, tools, tables, or framed artifacts rather than page-level decoration.

## Brand Taste Practical Tips

| Scenario | Decision | Source category |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The work feels polished but generic | Add a specific subject signal: product state, project artifact, material detail, person, place, proof point, or decision. | Let the subject lead |
| The composition feels empty | Remove low-value content first, then tighten grouping before adding decorative space or extra containers. | Restraint |
| A layout needs energy | Break the grid with one anchored move: image crop, quote, proof point, artifact, or number. Do not make several unrelated breaks. | Grid breaking |
| A piece feels too designed | Replace ornament with structure: stronger type hierarchy, better alignment, a thin rule, tonal surface, or more useful image. | Structure over ornament |
| The first read is unclear | Choose one dominant signal and demote supporting text, metadata, and secondary objects. | First-sight signal |
| Editorial mood is hiding the job | Raise the most useful information before adding mood. The work should stay sharp without becoming cryptic. | Editorial clarity |
| Repeated sections feel equally loud | Let only major turns use display scale; demote repeated headings, labels, and metadata. | Type hierarchy |
| Related elements feel disconnected | Pull related items closer, then separate the next group with a rule, larger space, or a page/slide break. | Negative space |
| Containment is becoming the default | Use open spacing, alignment, and thin rules unless the object is repeated, modular, tool-like, or physically framed. | Containment |
| A channel translation feels like a different brand | Carry over the same color roles, type restraint, image logic, and spacing rhythm before adapting production details. | Channel consistency |

## Type Hierarchy

Use a small set of distinct levels: understated label, measured body copy, large section or cover heading, quiet list text, and compact metadata. A hierarchy is successful when the reader knows where to start without every heading becoming oversized.

Major large headlines may use tight negative letter spacing. This helps large Indivisible headlines feel dense, confident, and editorial. Do not apply this behavior to body copy, metadata, button labels, table text, captions, footers, or compact production text.

Sentence case remains the default. Uppercase may appear in terse technical readouts, coordinates, codes, or artifact-derived labels when it improves operational interpretation. It should not be used to make ordinary navigation, buttons, headings, or metadata feel more important.

### Type Hierarchy Decision Flowchart

```text
Is this the main idea, cover statement, or major section turn?
|-- Yes -> Use a large headline with tight tracking
`-- No
    |-- Is it a repeated item title, phase title, row title, or object label?
    |   `-- Yes -> Use a restrained subhead
    |-- Is it metadata, status, caption, annotation, or a small label?
    |   `-- Yes -> Use compact quiet text with normal tracking
    `-- Is it long-form reading copy?
        `-- Yes -> Use measured body text with generous line height
```

### Practical Tips

| Scenario | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| A title feels weak at large size | Tighten tracking slightly and reduce decorative styling before increasing size |
| Every section feels equally loud | Demote repeated headings and let only major turns use display scale |
| Body copy feels hard to read | Reduce measure before changing the typeface |
| Metadata feels noisy | Use tertiary color, smaller size, and normal tracking |
| A heading wraps awkwardly | Adjust measure or line breaks before shrinking it dramatically |

## Negative Space

Negative space should create pace and confidence, not accidental gaps. Use large separation for major turns, tighter spacing for related content, and visible grouping for operational or reference information.

White space is working when the piece feels easier to scan. It is failing when related elements feel disconnected or when the audience has to infer relationships.

### Negative Space Decision Flowchart

```text
Are two elements part of the same thought, task, or reading unit?
|-- Yes -> Keep them close and separate them from the next group
`-- No
    |-- Is the next moment a major narrative or visual turn?
    |   `-- Yes -> Use large space, a rule, a page break, or a slide break
    |-- Is it a continuation of the same section?
    |   `-- Yes -> Use a moderate stack gap
    `-- Is the piece feeling dense?
        `-- Yes -> Remove low-value content before adding decorative space
```

## Alignment

Every output needs a clear alignment system. The exact grid changes by channel, but the edges should feel deliberate. Internal columns belong to their local section, spread, slide, frame, or component only.

Avoid mixing unrelated column systems in the same view or page. A row of objects should start and end on the same field as the text that introduces it unless there is a clear reason to break alignment.

### Alignment Decision Flowchart

```text
Does this element share the same role as nearby elements?
|-- Yes -> Use the same edge, field, or grid
`-- No
    |-- Is there a clear reason to expand, crop, bleed, inset, or isolate it?
    |   `-- Yes -> Use an intentional width or position tier
    `-- No -> Return to the shared alignment system
```

## When to Break the Grid

Break the grid only when it increases meaning, pace, or emphasis. Asymmetry should feel intentional and anchored. Do not break the grid to make a thin idea look designed.

Good reasons to break the grid:

- A strong image needs to create a first-sight signal.
- A case study, slide, poster, or spread needs a memorable editorial turn.
- A quote, proof point, artifact, or number deserves a pause.
- The composition has become too regular and needs controlled rhythm.

Bad reasons to break the grid:

- The content is thin.
- The composition lacks a clear message.
- Decoration is compensating for weak structure.
- The medium already imposes enough complexity.

## First-Sight Signal

Every piece needs an intentional first read. This can be a hero image, a cover line, a compact data summary, a single proof point, a strong title, or a quiet but precise opening.

### First-Sight Decision Flowchart

```text
Does the output need an immediate brand, person, place, product, or story signal?
|-- Yes -> Use image, type, artifact, or data as the dominant first read
`-- No
    |-- Is the output operational, instructional, or reference-driven?
    |   `-- Yes -> Start with title, metadata, and the most useful information
    |-- Is the content short and direct?
    |   `-- Yes -> Use a compact intro
    `-- Default -> Use a measured editorial lead, not a decorative opening
```

## Containment Decision Flowchart

```text
Is the element a repeated object, contained tool, physical card, modular block, or framed artifact?
|-- Yes -> Containment may be appropriate
`-- No
    |-- Can spacing, type, and a thin rule create the structure?
    |   `-- Yes -> Do not add a box
    |-- Is containment necessary for comprehension?
    |   `-- Yes -> Use a quiet surface, rule, or frame
    `-- Default -> Keep the layout open
```

Cards, panels, boxes, and frames should not become the default composition. Use them when they improve comprehension, modularity, or production.

## Imagery

Use imagery when it reveals the subject, proof, material, place, person, product, state, or mood. Imagery should feel specific, not stock-like. Favor natural texture, shadow, depth, and documentary relevance.

Do not use generic abstract vector art as a substitute for meaning. Do not crop important subject matter so aggressively that the work loses its point.

### Imagery Decision Flowchart

```text
Would an image reveal the subject, proof, material, place, person, or mood?
|-- Yes -> Use a strong raster image in a deliberate position
`-- No
    |-- Is the image only decorative?
    |   `-- Yes -> Omit it
    `-- Is the piece too dry without visual evidence?
        `-- Yes -> Use a project artifact, product state, material detail, or documentary image
```

## Density

Density should match the job. Brand and editorial work can use more pause. Reports, dashboards, decks, and reference pieces often need higher information density, but they should still feel aligned, quiet, and intentionally paced.

Do not make important operational information harder to use in pursuit of mood. Do not make editorial work feel generic in pursuit of efficiency.

## Universal Do / Don't

Do:

- Let image, type, spacing, material, and contrast carry the experience.
- Use asymmetry when it clarifies rhythm or emphasis.
- Keep long body copy in a controlled measure.
- Use muted text with selected primary emphasis for leading phrases or key terms.
- Let thin rules create structure.
- Keep the result useful even when it has editorial mood.

Don't:

- Add decorative gradients, bokeh blobs, ornamental vectors, generic icon grids, decorative vertical guide lines, or visible grid overlays.
- Add a large opening moment by default.
- Stretch editorial copy across the full available field.
- Use orange as a broad color theme.
- Treat every channel as a separate brand.
